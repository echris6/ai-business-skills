# Marketing

Attention is the scarce thing, and where attention goes, money follows. This skill gets the offer in front of the right people through three routes: organic posting, paid ads and direct outreach.

The mistake that ends most attempts: someone builds a product, posts one piece of content, gets nothing and quits. Marketing works as a testing system. One post proves nothing and twenty variants show exactly what the audience responds to.

## Where this skill sits

This is the seventh of eight skills that work as a chain. Before planning anything, read `business/icp.md` (the person, their channels, their language) and `business/context.md` (the offer, the tone, the proof, the rules and the what-worked log). The writing skill produces the words, the design skill produces the visuals, and the sales skill takes over when someone replies.

## Pick the route

- **Organic first.** It is free and it shows which message works before any money is spent.
- **Paid ads second.** Ads scale a message that organic has already proven. Ads on an unproven message spend money to learn what a free post would have shown.
- **Outreach when the buyers can be listed.** If the customers are businesses or a group that can be found in public data, go to them directly.

The channel comes from the ICP file. Younger audiences are on TikTok and Instagram, older ones on Facebook, gamers on YouTube and Twitch, and people looking for a method are on Reddit and YouTube search. Go where that person already scrolls, and don't pick a platform because it is popular.

## Three rules for every route

1. **Give real value.** Free content contains the whole method. The people who do it themselves were never going to buy, and the people who want the result without the work reach out.
2. **Test variants.** Publish several versions in different styles, read the results, keep the top three formats and keep testing new ones on the side.
3. **A human approves.** The agent researches, drafts and queues. A person posts, sends and turns ads on, unless the user has clearly said otherwise for a specific channel.

## Organic: Reddit

The method: **give away the entire method in the post and make the sale in the DMs.** A post built this way did $1,000 a day for a SaaS from the first post, and posts from the same method still bring sales DMs to an info product from Google traffic almost two years later.

Why it works: Reddit's front page filters for generosity. A complete method with real numbers reads as a gift and gets upvoted. Anything with a hook, a link or a held-back step reads as marketing and dies. The post creates two groups, the people who will do the method by hand and the people who want the outcome without the work, and the second group sends a DM.

**Rules that override everything else**

- The post holds nothing back. Every step, real numbers, complete enough that a reader could do it today.
- No link, no product name and no "DM me" in the post.
- A human clicks submit on every post, comment and DM.
- Every number is the user's real number. Comment sections spot inflated figures fast, and one caught lie ends the account's credibility.

**Subreddits**

- Go where the buyer wants the outcome, not where the product category is discussed. A tool for contractors sells in contractor subreddits, not in r/SaaS. For make-money offers, r/sidehustle and r/passive_income have produced posts with millions of views.
- Check each subreddit's posting requirements first: minimum karma, minimum account age, flair rules.
- Read the top posts of the last year in each one. Note which formats win, the usual length, how people talk and what got removed. Keep a short notes file per subreddit and update it every time.
- Start with three to five. Three done well beat ten done generically.

**Accounts**

- An account needs both age and karma. An old account with no karma still can't post in most good subreddits, so buying aged accounts alone doesn't work. Followers don't matter on Reddit.
- Warm every account before its first post with a couple of weeks of real comments and answers in the target subreddits. This builds karma and teaches the tone.
- Rotate. Run several accounts and post from a different one each day. The same account posting daily in the same subreddits gets flagged. Keep a log per account: subreddits used, last post date, karma.
- What gets accounts banned: automated posting through VPNs and proxies, and automated DM sending. Don't retry either with better proxies. Losing DM ability means losing the sales channel.
- What doesn't: agent-drafted, human-sent everything.

**The post**

1. Title: the real result, plainly, phrased the way winning titles are phrased in that subreddit.
2. Open with the result and how long it took.
3. How it started, briefly, including what failed first.
4. The complete method, step by step, with the actual tools, templates and order.
5. Answers to the doubts a skeptic would raise. Objections answered become upvoted comments, and objections ignored become the top comment calling it fake.
6. End on the last step.

Write a separate version per subreddit. Identical text across subreddits and accounts is a detection pattern.

**Monitoring.** Reddit's public RSS (add `.rss` to a subreddit or thread URL, about one request a minute) lets the agent watch for people describing the exact problem, asking for recommendations or complaining about a competitor. Draft a reply that fully answers each one. The product comes up only when it is the honest answer, and usually only in DMs after the person replies.

**The long tail.** Good posts rank on Google and keep selling for years, so write titles for two readers: the subreddit today and the person searching next year. Where they conflict, the subreddit wins.

**Scaling.** The system scales by adding hands, not automation: more warmed accounts in rotation, and freelancers who post the queued drafts and send the queued DMs. The agent stays the single brain with one set of notes and one queue.

## Organic: short-form video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

Before scripting, collect 20 to 30 top videos in the niche (the research skill does this) and note each one's hook, on-screen text, format and length.

**The four parts.** A script missing one gets rewritten, not posted.

1. **Hook (first 3 seconds).** Half of viewers leave here. The hook is the single line that makes scrolling past feel like a loss. It is not an introduction and not the brand name.
   - Curiosity: "I didn't believe this worked until I tried it"
   - Blunt statement: "Stop doing [common thing]. Here's why"
   - Result: "This [did specific result] while I slept"
   - Confession: "I wasted [real number] on [thing] so you don't have to"
   - Enemy: "[Common advice] is why you're stuck"

   A specific number beats a vague claim, and the viewer's own words beat marketing language. The on-screen text adds a second hook and doesn't caption the spoken one.
2. **Retention (3 seconds to the end).** Completion rate drives reach. Cut every two to three seconds, change something every 10 to 15 seconds (a new visual, a sound, a text pop), open a loop in the middle ("the best part is coming"), and never use wording that signals the end.
3. **Reward.** Deliver what the hook promised, completely, with the payoff at the end. Know which kind it is: teaching the actual thing, a real before and after, entertainment, or a demo of the product doing the job on screen.
4. **Engagement prompt (last 3 to 5 seconds).** One easy ask: a one-word comment ("drop a 'yes' if you want the link"), a save or a tag. One per video.

The script format, with cut markers, is in the writing skill. A script without cut markers isn't finished.

**Five content types to rotate**

1. The pain shown in full, then the better way.
2. A real demo with a real screen and real output.
3. One complete, usable tactic.
4. The founder's story with a real low point.
5. Old way against new way, side by side.

**Platform notes**

- TikTok: fastest pacing, raw production wins, hooks can be blunter.
- Reels: a little more polish is fine, and shares to stories drive distribution.
- Shorts: feeds the main channel, and endings that loop back into the first line get rewatched.
- Cross-post everything, and remove watermarks first because platforms suppress each other's marks.

**The testing system**

- Two different hooks for every video, posted as separate videos.
- Vary formats, tones and content types on purpose. Find the top three by response and lean on them while still testing new ones.
- No conclusions before 20 posts.
- Read the right numbers: 3-second hold for the hook, completion rate for the content, shares and saves for reach, comments and profile taps for buying interest.

**Diagnosing a weak video.** Low 3-second hold is a hook problem. Early drop-off is pacing. Full watches with no engagement is a missing prompt. Views with no follows or sales is a reward or offer problem. Rewrite the weakest part first.

**Turning it into money.** The plug goes in the engagement prompt, the pinned comment and the bio. A comment answered with real help and then the offer in DMs outsells any link. Never gate the value itself.

## Organic: YouTube

**Title and thumbnail come before the video exists.** If no clickable title and thumbnail can be found for an idea, the video doesn't get made. This one rule saves more production time than everything else.

**Ideas** come from outliers: videos in the niche that did 3x their channel's median or more (the research skill builds this list), and from the recurring questions in their comments. An idea with no relative anywhere in the niche's outliers needs a strong reason to exist.

**Title**

- Write ten or more per idea. Keep the one a viewer could repeat to a friend in one sentence ("did you see the one where...").
- The title says what the viewer gets and why they'd care, readable in two seconds.
- Title and thumbnail never say the same thing. Each adds what the other lacks.
- Use the audience's own words from the comments. Put the words that sell the click first, since titles get cut off around 50 to 60 characters.
- Be ready to retitle a live video that gets impressions but few clicks.

**Thumbnail.** It gets about two seconds before the video starts playing on its own, and then the first five seconds of the video have to earn the click again. It should open a question only the video answers. The design skill has the full rules. Make two or three different concepts, and test two per video with YouTube's Test & Compare.

**Script, in six beats**

1. The hook: the first five seconds restate the promise. Never open on an intro or "hey guys".
2. A reason to stay: open a second loop and start delivering fast, because viewers decide again at 30 to 60 seconds.
3. The build-up toward the payoff.
4. The payoff, delivered in full.
5. One extra beat after the payoff, which trains viewers to watch to the end.
6. A wrap-up that lasts seconds and points at the next video.

Plan a visual change every 20 to 40 seconds of talking, and never use wording that signals the end before the end.

**Reading the data**

- Find where the first 30% of viewers left. That is usually in the first minute and it is a hook or expectation problem.
- Find where half were gone, watch that exact section and name what caused it.
- Study the bumps too. A rise in the retention graph is a moment that worked, so do more of it.
- A cliff in the first 15 seconds means the video didn't match its title and thumbnail. A slow steady decline is pacing. A sharp drop at one timestamp is one mistake.
- Rising impressions with falling click-through usually means the video is being shown to new audiences.
- Review at the first hour, first day, first week and 30 days, and compare only against the channel's own history.

## Paid ads

Start when an organic message has clearly worked and the checkout converts (the sales skill sets that up).

### The main principles

1. **Ads multiply what already works.** They don't fix an offer nobody wanted. Take the message that performed best organically and put money behind that.
2. **Know the numbers before spending.** Write down the price, what it costs to deliver one sale, and so the most that can be paid to get one sale. That figure decides everything else.
3. **The creative does most of the work.** The image or video and its first line decide who stops and who buys, more than any targeting setting. Start with a broad audience and let the creative call out the buyer.
4. **Test ideas, not small tweaks.** Run several clearly different creatives: different hooks, different formats, different angles on the same offer. A button color is not a test.
5. **Set the rules before launch.** With the user, agree the daily budget and when a creative gets turned off. A simple rule: when a creative has spent the most you would pay for one sale and has no sale, it goes off. Nobody judges well in the middle of a campaign.
6. **Give it time, then act.** Don't change a new ad every few hours. Let it gather enough results to judge against the rule, then cut or keep.
7. **Scale winners slowly and keep feeding new creatives.** Raise budget in steps. Every ad wears out as the same people see it again, so new variants of the winner are always in the queue.
8. **Match the page to the ad.** The landing page headline repeats the promise that earned the click.
9. **Judge by sales, not clicks.** Cheap clicks that don't buy are worthless. Track purchases back to the ad that caused them.
10. **Read the chain to find the break.** Few people stopping is a hook problem. People stopping but not clicking is an offer or body problem. Clicks without purchases is a landing page or price problem.
11. **Stay honest.** No income claims that can't be verified, no invented testimonials, no fake deadlines. They get ads rejected and accounts closed.

### Where the creatives come from

- The best-performing organic posts and videos, reused as ads.
- First lines taken from the ICP file's language bank.
- The research skill's notes on competitor ads that have run for months.
- Customer quotes and real results from the Proof section of the context file.

The design skill makes the creatives in the right sizes and the writing skill writes the copy. Ask for at least four to six clearly different creatives for a first test.

### Whop Ads

Whop Ads lets a business that sells on Whop run ads from inside its Whop dashboard. Checked against Whop's documentation at the time of writing:

- **What it is:** agency ad accounts plus Whop's own tracking pixel, managed from one dashboard. Whop supplies the setup. The strategy, the creatives and the campaigns are still the user's job.
- **Where the ads run:** Facebook and Instagram. Other platforms are listed as coming later.
- **Cost:** no setup fee, no monthly fee and no minimum spend. The user pays for the ad spend.
- **Setup:** open the Ads tab in the Whop business dashboard, connect the Facebook page and Instagram account, and Whop creates a pixel for the business that already knows its past Whop sales. Existing audiences from another ad account can be imported. Then launch the first campaign. There is one ad account per Whop business.
- **Paying for spend:** a credit card, a Whop Card, or the pending Whop balance.
- **Tracking:** the Whop pixel uses the actual payment data, so a sale is counted when someone pays. It also passes purchases back to Meta, and it connects to outside tracking tools.
- **Team access:** a media buyer or an agent's operator can be invited with the Advertiser role, which opens the Ads dashboard and nothing else.
- **Review:** creatives and copy are checked against Meta's rules before launch. Income claims that can't be verified, fake testimonials, adult content and deceptive offers are not allowed.

How to use it with this skill:

1. Confirm the product, the price and the checkout are live on Whop.
2. Agree the numbers and the rules from the principles above.
3. Build one campaign with the test creatives against a broad audience, and leave it paused.
4. Show the user the creatives, the copy, the budget and the rules, and wait for approval before anything is switched on.
5. Check results against the rules on the schedule agreed, turn off what fails, and report what was spent and what was sold.
6. Send the winner's pattern to the design and writing skills for new variants, and log it.

If the user doesn't sell on Whop, the same principles apply in Meta's own Ads Manager or any other ad platform.

## Outreach

For businesses and any buyer that can be found in public data.

1. **Build the list from public data.** `compass/crawler-google-places` on Apify for local businesses by trade and city, `apify/facebook-ads-scraper` for companies running ads, and directories and job boards for the rest.
2. **Qualify by a visible need.** Keep only the ones where the problem can be seen from outside: reviews saying nobody answered the phone, an ad that hasn't changed in a year, a site with no way to book. A list of everyone is a list of nobody.
3. **Do the work first.** Make a sample for that specific business (a rebuilt page, a new ad, a short audit) and lead with it. A message that shows finished work made for them gets read, and a message that describes a service gets deleted.
4. **Pick the channel by the contact data available:** email, SMS or a postcard. In the author's own tests, postcards outperformed email and SMS for local businesses.
5. **Keep the message short.** Who you are, what you noticed, the sample, one easy question. Write it in the words of their trade.
6. **Follow up with something new each time**, and stop when they say no.
7. **Stay inside the rules.** Include a way to opt out, send SMS only where it is allowed, and keep volume per sending account low.

Replies go to the sales skill.

## Log everything

After every post, ad test or outreach batch, add an entry to the what-worked log in `business/context.md`: what it was, the result and whether to repeat it. Update the top three formats when the ranking changes. This log is what makes month three better than month one.

## Running this skill

When the user asks how to get customers: read the files, pick the route and the primary channel from the ICP, and deliver a plan for the first 20 posts, or the first ad test, or the first 50 prospects, with the drafts requested from the writing and design skills. When this runs as an ongoing system: deliver a daily brief with new buying-intent posts and a drafted reply for each, the posts scheduled for the day ready to paste, ad results against the rules that were set, and updates to the log. When the user says marketing isn't working: find the stage that fails. No views is a hook or channel problem, views without replies is a value or offer problem, and replies without sales belongs to the sales skill.
