# Writing

Good copy is mostly research. The customer already said what they want, in their words, and the business already has a voice. The frameworks in this skill only arrange what the research found. Copy built this way converts because readers recognise themselves in it.

## Where this skill sits

This is the fifth of eight skills that work as a chain. Before writing, read:

- `business/context.md` for the tone rules, the cut list, the samples, the proof and the offer.
- `business/icp.md` for the person being written to, their awareness, their objections and the language bank.
- `business/research.md` when a piece needs real quotes or the ranking of what people care about.

If the tone section is missing, ask for two or three samples of the user's own writing and extract the rules first (the context skill explains how). If there is no research at all, build a starter language bank from Reddit and reviews before writing, because copy without it is guessing.

## The rules for every piece

1. **Write to one person.** The ICP, on the channel they're on, at the moment in their day they'll read it.
2. **Use their words.** Stop paraphrasing the audience and start quoting them. If the customer says "get more calls", the page does not say "increase inbound lead volume". When a headline is needed, the best one is usually already in the language bank.
3. **Be specific.** "Save $3,500 in 16 weeks" beats "save money". "Join 46,700 others" beats "join thousands". Exact figures are more believable than round ones.
4. **Real numbers only.** Every figure comes from the context file. If the number isn't there, leave a `[placeholder]` and ask. Invented specifics are dishonest and they get caught.
5. **Lead with the feeling, back it with facts.** People decide on how something feels and then look for reasons, so open on the felt problem or the wanted result and follow with the numbers and details they will use to justify it.
6. **Outcomes before features.** Run every feature through "so what?" twice. Memory foam. So what? It shapes to your body. So what? You wake up without the back pain. The copy leads with the last answer and uses the first as proof.
7. **One job per piece.** A post earns attention, a page earns the purchase, an email earns the click. A piece that tries to do all of them does none.
8. **Give the value away.** Free content contains the full method and holds nothing back. Generous content gets shared and builds the audience that buys.
9. **Say how it works.** State the actual mechanism in everyday words. A claim with no mechanism reads as hype.
10. **Keep it simple.** Short sentences in active voice, one idea each, in the reader's own level of language. The main message lands in the first three seconds of reading.
11. **Trust beats pressure.** Honest claims, precise numbers and admitted limits win over skeptics that hype loses. Use a deadline or a limit only when it is real.

## Position before writing

- **Find the gap.** List what competitors claim and what their bad reviews say. Where "what people dislike about the alternatives" meets "what this product does well" is the positioning, and often the headline.
- **One big idea per piece.** A page or an email sells one change. Everything else is cut or moved to another piece.
- **Match their awareness.** Readers who only feel the problem need it named first. Readers comparing solutions need to see what is different. Readers who know the product need proof and an offer. The ICP file says which stage each channel's audience is in.

## The frameworks

Frameworks arrange the research. They never replace it.

**PAS (problem, agitate, solution)** for landing pages, ads and cold outreach. Name the problem in the audience's exact words, make the cost of not solving it concrete (time, money, the bad moment in their day), then present the solution with proof. If the middle part sounds like the Reddit thread it came from, it is working.

**AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action)** for longer pages and email sequences. A specific headline, then story and relevant facts, then the result and the proof, then one clear call to action.

**FAB (feature, advantage, benefit)** for product sections and comparisons. State the fact, what it makes possible and the felt result, always ending on the result.

**Story-led** for founder brands and warm audiences. The founder had the reader's exact problem, tried what the reader has tried, and found what the reader is now looking at. Keep it true.

## Weak and strong

| Weak | Strong | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Powerful lead generation for modern businesses | Finds 100 local leads in ten minutes | A number and a result |
| Tired of no-shows? | "Third no-show this week, that's $180 I'm never getting back" | A real quote beats a guessed question |
| Our AI-powered platform streamlines your workflow | It reads each paid order and sends it to your supplier | Says what it does |
| Sign up today | See your first report in 2 minutes | Says what happens next |
| Trusted by thousands | 1,240 groomers use it every week | Exact count |

The figures in the table are placeholders to show the shape. Real copy uses the user's numbers.

## Headlines and hooks

Most readers never get past the headline, so it gets a large share of the effort.

Check every headline against four qualities: useful (a clear benefit), urgent (a reason to care now), unique (something a competitor couldn't also say) and specific (numbers and concrete detail).

Shapes that work:

- The result: "[specific result] in [time]"
- How to with the obstacle removed: "how to [result] without [the thing they dread]"
- The question the customer already asks, word for word from the language bank
- The problem called out: "still [problem] every month?"
- The mistake: "stop doing [common thing], do this"
- The confession: "I wasted [real number] on [thing]"
- A customer's own sentence used as the headline
- A number list: "7 ways to [outcome]", with digits

Write ten or more for every piece and shortlist two or three. Hooks get tested, not chosen, so always deliver more than one.

## Calls to action

- One per goal.
- Start with a verb: get, start, see, claim.
- Say what happens next: "see your first report in 2 minutes".
- Use lower-commitment wording where it is honest. "Buy" and "submit" add friction.
- Repeat the same call to action down a long page and never change its promise.

## Proof

- Real people with names and specifics beat anonymous praise.
- Put each piece of proof next to the claim it supports, not in a wall of testimonials.
- The strongest proof answers the top objection from the ICP file.
- If there is no proof yet, don't manufacture any. Lean on specifics, a guarantee and honesty, and say plainly that it is early.

## Sounding like a person

These patterns make text read as machine-written. Leave them out unless the user's own samples use them:

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and "Most people do X. This is different."
- Lists of exactly three adjectives or three short parallel sentences.
- One-line fragments placed for drama.
- A closing line that restates the paragraph in a clever way.
- Hype words: unlock, elevate, game-changing, seamless, revolutionize, supercharge.
- Openers that announce what is about to be said, and endings that summarise what was just said.
- Em dashes and heavy formatting in channels where people write plainly.
- Comparisons and metaphors where a plain statement would do.

Then apply the cut list from the context file, which outranks everything in this section. It records what this user has already removed from past drafts.

Sentences should connect with ordinary words like so, and, because and but. Read the draft aloud. Anything that sounds strange spoken gets rewritten.

## Formats

### Landing page or sales page

1. Headline and subhead: the one big idea, who it is for and how it works.
2. Proof near the top: a real result, a screenshot or a customer quote.
3. The problem and its cost, in their words (PAS).
4. How it works, in three to five steps.
5. What they get, each item ending on its result (FAB).
6. Questions: the real objections from the ICP file, each answered honestly.
7. More proof.
8. Price and promise.
9. One call to action, repeated after the first proof, after the price and at the end.

### Free value post (Reddit and similar)

1. Title: the real result, stated plainly, in the way that community phrases its best titles.
2. Open with the result and how long it took.
3. How it started, briefly, including what failed first.
4. The complete method, step by step, with the actual tools, templates and order.
5. Answers to the doubts a skeptic would raise.
6. End on the last step. No link, no product name, no "DM me" and no sign-off. The completeness is what makes people reach out.

Tone: first person, written like someone typing, minimal formatting, real numbers stated plainly. Write a separate version for each community.

### Short-form video script

```
Reel: [name]
Length: [15 to 60 seconds, 25 to 35 is the usual best]
Format: [screen recording / talking head / voiceover and demo / split screen]

Hook (0 to 3 sec):
[exact first line, plus what is on screen]

Body:
[line]
[Cut: visual direction]
[line]
[Cut: visual direction]

Engagement prompt (last 3 to 5 sec):
[exact line]

On-screen text: [overlays, timed]
```

Cut markers are part of the script. Write two hooks for every script. The marketing skill has the full method.

### Long video script

Six beats: a hook in the first five seconds that restates the title's promise, a reason to stay, the build-up, the payoff, one extra beat after the payoff, and a wrap-up that lasts seconds. Write for the ear and mark a visual change every 20 to 40 seconds.

### Emails

- One idea and one link per email.
- The subject line follows the headline rules, with curiosity weighted higher.
- Plain text reads as personal and usually does better than a designed layout.
- Welcome email: deliver what was promised and say what comes next.
- Sales sequence across several sends: the problem, why it costs them, the solution, the proof, the offer.
- Newsletter: useful material in the niche every time, with the product mentioned as the shortcut.

### Ad copy

The first line is the hook, because feeds cut the rest off. One problem or one wanted result per ad. The audience's own phrasing matters most here, since people seeing an ad know the least about you. Then proof, the offer and the call to action. Write several versions that differ in the first line.

### Cold outreach

The shortest format. A first line that proves it was written for them, one thing you noticed, one piece of proof or a sample, and one easy question. A message in the words of the prospect's own trade reads as a peer writing and not as a pitch.

### Product descriptions and replies

Descriptions state what it is, who it is for and what they get, in that order. For replies to leads, use the sales skill, which owns the conversation flow.

## Check before delivering

- Would the ICP recognise their own words in the first line?
- Is every number in the context file?
- Could any sentence be cut without losing information? Cut it.
- Does every feature end on a result?
- Does it match the samples in the context file when read side by side?
- Is anything from the cut list in it?
- Does the piece do its one job, with one call to action?

## After the user edits

Compare their version to the draft, add what they cut or changed to the cut list in `business/context.md`, and build the next draft from their version. Never bring back something they removed.

## Running this skill

When the user asks for a piece of writing: confirm the format and where it will be published, read the three files, pick the framework by format and awareness stage, and deliver the piece with two or three headline or hook options. Show the research behind it by noting which phrases from the language bank were used and which objections the copy answers, so the user can judge it. When the user brings copy that isn't working: check it against the rules (language the customer wouldn't use, vagueness, features with no result, a buried call to action, missing proof), name what it breaks, and rewrite those parts. When the user says it doesn't sound like them: put the draft next to their samples, find the differences, fix the draft and update the tone rules.
