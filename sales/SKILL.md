# Sales

Marketing gets someone interested. This skill covers everything between that moment and the payment, and what happens after it. Most small businesses lose sales here for simple reasons: there is no easy way to pay, replies are slow, the pitch comes too early, and nobody follows up.

## Where this skill sits

This is the last of eight skills that work as a chain. Before replying to anyone, read `business/icp.md` (the person and their objections), `business/context.md` (the tone, the proof, the honest limits and the rules) and `business/offer.md` (the offer, the price, the delivery steps and the refund rule). This skill keeps its own file, `business/leads.md`.

## Step 1: the checkout comes first

Nothing gets promoted until a stranger can pay in under a minute.

- Create a payment link or checkout page with a provider such as Whop, Stripe, Gumroad or Lemon Squeezy. One product, one price, one button. Don't build a custom checkout.
- For a monthly service, set it up as a subscription so nobody is invoiced by hand.
- For physical or custom work, collect what delivery needs at checkout: an address, a size, the files you need from them.
- If the offer has tiers, show two or three at most, with the one most people should buy in the middle.
- Buy it yourself once, on a phone. Fix every step that confused you.
- Set the confirmation message: what they bought, what happens next and where to get help. Delivery follows the steps in the offer file.

Put the link in `business/context.md` so every skill uses the same one.

## Step 2: the conversation

For a small business the conversation is the checkout page. A DM, a comment thread and an email reply all follow the same flow.

1. **Never open with the pitch.** The person reached out because something was useful. The first reply is more of that: answer their actual question fully.
2. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Ask what they're working with: their situation, what they've tried, what they want to happen. One or two questions, not a form.
3. **Offer the product as the shortcut.** After the real answer, one line with no pressure: "if you'd rather skip the manual part, this is what I built for it."
4. **State the price plainly** when they ask or show interest, and send the link.
5. **If it isn't a fit, say so** and point them to something that is. A wrong-fit sale turns into a refund and a public complaint.

### An example

A made-up exchange to show the shape. Someone messages after a post about getting cleaning clients: "hey, saw your post. does this work if I'm just starting and have zero reviews?"

Weak reply:

```
Hey! Yes it works great for beginners. I have a full guide that covers everything for $29,
here's the link: [link]. Let me know if you have questions!
```

It pitches before answering, and the person learns nothing from it.

Strong reply:

```
yeah it does, zero reviews is where everyone starts. do the first 3 jobs for people you know
at half price and ask each one for a google review before you leave the house, that gets you
past the empty profile in about two weeks.

are you going after homes or offices? the next step is different for each.
```

Then, after they answer and get another real answer:

```
that's the whole method, you can run it from here. if you'd rather have it laid out step by step
with the scripts and the price sheet, i put it all in a guide, it's $29. happy to send the link.
```

The strong version answers the question, asks one thing, and offers the product only after giving real help. The person can say no and still think well of the seller, which matters because many buy later.

### Rules for replies

- Speed matters more than polish. A good reply within the hour beats a perfect one tomorrow, so the agent drafts replies as messages arrive and queues them for the user to send.
- Write in the tone from the context file and use the person's own words back to them.
- Answer the question they asked, not the one that leads to the pitch.
- One message, one purpose. Don't stack three questions and a link.
- Never claim anything outside the honest limits in the context file.

### Comments

A public comment gets a full public answer, because everyone else reading has the same question. The offer stays out of the thread unless someone asks directly. People who want more will message.

## Step 3: objections

Keep a table in `business/leads.md`:

| Objection (their words) | What is behind it | Honest answer | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Start it from the objections in the ICP file and add every new one from a real conversation. Most fall into five groups:

- **Price:** "that's a lot for a pdf." Behind it is doubt about the result. Answer with what the result is worth and what they already pay for worse options, and offer the smaller tier if there is one.
- **Trust:** "how do I know this works?" Answer with a specific result and where it came from, and with the guarantee.
- **Fit:** "will it work for my situation?" Answer honestly. If yes, say why with a similar case. If no, say no.
- **Time:** "I don't have time for this." Answer with how long the first step takes.
- **Someone else decides:** "I need to ask my partner." Give them a short summary they can forward.

Rules:

- Answer with facts and proof from the context file.
- Don't argue and don't discount on the spot. A discount offered under pressure teaches people to push.
- "Not now" is an answer. Log it and follow up later with something new.
- When the same objection appears several times, send it to the writing skill so the landing page answers it before anyone has to ask.

## Step 4: follow-ups

Most people don't buy in the first conversation and most sellers never write again. Follow-ups on warm conversations convert better than any new post, so these get drafted before new content does.

- **Every follow-up adds something new:** an answer to a question they raised, a result from another customer, a short example made for their situation. "Just checking in" adds nothing.
- **Default rhythm:** a few days after the first conversation, a week after that, then one last message two weeks later. The user can change the rhythm, and it goes in the context file's rules.
- **Stop** the moment they say no, and after the last message either way.

Examples of follow-ups that add something:

```
quick one, you asked about offices last week. someone just used the same script on a dental
practice and got the contract, so it does carry over. happy to share what they changed.
```

```
made you a one-page version of the price sheet for homes only, since that's what you're doing.
[attachment]
```

## Step 5: the leads log

`business/leads.md` holds one line per person:

```
[name or handle] | [source: which post, ad or outreach batch] | [what they asked] | [stage] | [next action and date]
```

Stages: new, answered, offered, follow-up 1, follow-up 2, follow-up 3, bought, not a fit, said no.

The log makes sure nobody is forgotten and nobody is messaged twice by accident. It also shows which posts and channels bring buyers and not only replies, and that goes back to the marketing skill.

Once a week, count how many people are at each stage. The stage where the count piles up is the stage to fix.

## Higher-priced offers: calls and proposals

When the price needs a conversation:

**Call outline**

1. Their situation now.
2. What they've tried and why it didn't work.
3. What the result would be worth to them.
4. How the offer gets them there, in the order they'd experience it.
5. The price, said once and plainly.
6. The next step and when it happens.

Spend most of the call on the first three. Let them talk. The agent can prepare the call by pulling everything known about the lead from the log and the research.

**Proposal.** One page, sent the same day with the payment link:

```
## What you told me
[their problem, in their words]

## What I'll deliver
[the list, with dates]

## What I need from you
## Price
## How to start
[payment link, and what happens the day after]
```

For services sold through outreach, a finished sample made for that business does more than any proposal. Lead with it.

## After the sale

- **Deliver straight away** using the steps in the offer file.
- **Check in** once they've had time to use it. Fix problems before they become refund requests.
- **Ask for the result.** When a customer gets the outcome, ask for the number or a sentence about it and for permission to use it. Add it to Proof in the context file, with the real name and details where they agree.
- **Offer the next thing.** A buyer is the easiest person to sell to. Offer what they need next: the done-for-you version, the next tier or the monthly plan.
- **Put buyers on an email list.** Every buyer and every warm conversation goes on a list, with a free chapter, a template or included updates as the reason to join. Send useful material in the niche regularly, with the product and its higher tiers mentioned as the shortcut. This is what turns one-off sales into recurring income, and it is why the second product is always easier to sell than the first.
- **Refunds.** Follow the rule in the offer file, quickly and without argument, and write down the reason. Refund reasons are research.

## Feeding it back

Sales conversations are the best research the business will ever get:

- New objections and phrases go into `business/icp.md`.
- Questions that keep coming up go to the writing skill for the landing page and the next posts.
- Which sources produced buyers goes into the what-worked log in `business/context.md`.
- What buyers ask for after buying goes to the offer skill as the next product.

## Rules

- The agent drafts and a human sends on any platform that bans automated messages. Losing an account means losing the conversations in it.
- No invented urgency. A deadline or a limit is mentioned only when it is real.
- Every number and claim comes from the context file.
- Don't contact anyone who said no or asked to stop.
- Don't message the same person on two channels at once.

## When there is interest but few sales

Read the last twenty conversations and find where people stop:

- They never get a reply within a day: speed problem.
- They get a pitch in the first message and go quiet: the flow is wrong.
- They ask the price and vanish: the price was never connected to the result, or the price is far outside what the ICP file says they pay.
- They say "sounds good" and don't buy: the checkout has friction, or nobody followed up.
- They buy and refund: the offer promised more than the product delivers.

Fix that one stage before changing anything else.

## Running this skill

When the user is about to start marketing: set up and test the checkout first. When leads are coming in: deliver a daily brief with a drafted reply for every new message, the follow-ups due today and the updated leads log. When the user gets interest but few sales: run the diagnosis above. When the user closes a sale: log it, start delivery and schedule the check-in.
